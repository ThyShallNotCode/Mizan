Multiple-choice grading on your own computer, with every doubtful mark sent to a person.

02How it works

من المسح الضوئي إلى اعتماد النتائج

From a stack of scans to released results, with you deciding every doubt.

  1. 01

    Scan and set up

    Pick the folder of scans, the key and the roster; Mizan shows every value it will grade and where it came from.

  2. 02

    Read and score

    Mizan reads every mark without seeing the key, matches each sheet to a student and scores it.

  3. 03

    Review doubts

    Each doubtful mark appears as scanned: accept the proposal, or choose another answer and give a reason.

  4. 04

    Release results

    Release is your deliberate act; it is refused while anything is open and, when the exam has a roster, it writes the Blackboard upload.

  5. 05

    Export reports

    Scores and grades, item analysis, outcome tables and an HTML report, written to the exam’s folder when grading ends and again whenever you export.

What it reads

Scans

PDF or TIFF straight from the scanner, or JPEG and PNG files.

Answer sheets

The existing 100-question sheet, or sheets Mizan prints for an exam.

Key and roster

A key file, a scanned key sheet or Remark’s graded Excel export; a roster in CSV or Excel, with Arabic names.

03What you get

كلُّ إجابةٍ في ميزانها

Results, item analysis and outcome tables from one run.

Catches a probable wrong key.

Item analysis flags a question whose key the strongest students contradict. A confident flag holds release until you decide.

Outcomes for the course report.

With a file that maps questions to learning outcomes, it writes outcome tables for the NCAAA course report, with Arabic and English headers, in Excel, CSV and HTML. Scores carry Saudi letter grades.

It asks; it never guesses.

Faint or double marks, erasures, unreadable student numbers and students missing from the roster wait for a person. An unreadable answer is never counted as blank.

Review at keyboard speed.

Each doubt appears as scanned, with a proposal. Enter accepts it; a letter key picks another answer, with a reason; Ctrl+Z undoes; and the results update as you go.

It reads the exam for you.

Give it the scans, and the key and roster if you have them. It proposes the versions, the number of questions and the title, shows where each came from, and asks when the evidence disagrees.

Arabic where it matters.

Rosters with Arabic headers and names. Review keys work on the Arabic keyboard layout, and options can be typed as أ ب ج د هـ or in Arabic-Indic digits. The grade chart shows Arabic grade letters; the interface itself is in English.

04Trust, privacy and proof

دقّةٌ في التصحيح، وشفافيةٌ في القرار

Local by design, and on the record.

  • * On Windows, so far only network paths and Dropbox-style folders are refused.
  • Mizan does not encrypt these folders: keep them on an encrypted disk, and back up both.
  • “No network calls of its own” comes from a review of the program’s code, not from a traffic test.

Safeguards

  • Doubts wait for you. A doubtful mark is never guessed; an unreadable answer is never counted as blank.
  • No near-matches. A student is never matched to the nearest roster entry; a doubtful identity goes to review.
  • Overrides need a reason. Changing Mizan’s proposal requires a reason, and the reason is recorded.
  • Absent is not zero. An absent sheet is held for you, never scored zero.
  • A tamper-evident record. Every decision goes into a signed, hash-chained audit log, checked each time the exam opens.
  • Only a key you confirmed. An inferred key can never be released; a key sheet waits for your confirmation.
